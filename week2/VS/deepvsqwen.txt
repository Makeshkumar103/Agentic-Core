The reason isn't just preference—it comes down to how each method handles filtration and extraction. Pour-over uses a paper filter that traps nearly all oils and micro-fines, which leaves behind a cleaner cup where the bean's inherent bright acids, floral notes, and delicate sugars can shine without competition. It's also highly repeatable: once you lock in your grind size, water temperature (usually around 195–205°F), and pour velocity, the extraction window is narrow enough to dial in reliably. French press, meanwhile, keeps those oils and suspended particles intact, which gives a heavier mouthfeel and richer body, but it also introduces more variability. Without proper settling time or careful decanting, over-extraction can sneak in, and those fine grounds left in the cup can mute the subtler tasting notes you might be after.

So if the goal is precision, transparency of flavor, and day-to-day consistency, pour-over is the clearer winner.
